Quality beats quantity. Pick **one main course and one book** per area and finish them, rather than collecting twenty bookmarks. Everything below is free to read or watch (some books also have paid print editions).

## If you only pick three things

1. **Stanford CS231n** (deep learning for vision) for the modern core.
2. **Szeliski, *Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications*** (free online) as the classical reference you dip into.
3. **A project** of your own (module 18), because nothing teaches faster than debugging real data.

## Courses

| Course | Best for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| [Stanford CS231n: Deep Learning for Computer Vision](https://cs231n.stanford.edu) | CNNs, training, detection, segmentation, transformers, generative models | Notes, assignments and lecture videos; the standard interview background |
| [Michigan EECS 498-007 / 598-005: Deep Learning for Computer Vision](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~justincj/teaching/eecs498/) | A more detailed, modern version by Justin Johnson | Excellent lectures on backprop, CNN architectures, attention |
| [First Principles of Computer Vision (Shree Nayar, Columbia)](https://fpcv.cs.columbia.edu) | Image formation, cameras, optics, classical vision with clear intuition | Short videos in themed series |
| [Hugging Face Computer Vision Course](https://huggingface.co/learn/computer-vision-course) | Transformers for vision, foundation models, hands-on with `transformers` | Community course, practical |
| [fast.ai Practical Deep Learning](https://course.fast.ai) | Learning by building: train good models quickly | Top-down, code-first |
| [OpenCV tutorials](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/d9/df8/tutorial_root.html) | The library you will use daily | Official tutorials by module |
| [Cyrill Stachniss: Photogrammetry and Robotics lectures (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/@CyrillStachniss) | Camera geometry, SfM, SLAM, ICP, bundle adjustment, Kalman filters | Very clear derivations for robotics perception |
| [TUM Multiple View Geometry (Daniel Cremers)](https://cvg.cit.tum.de/teaching/online/mvg) | Rigorous 3-D vision | More mathematical |
| [MIT 6.S191 Introduction to Deep Learning](https://introtodeeplearning.com) | A fast tour of deep learning | Annual lectures and labs |

## Books

| Book | Level | What it gives you |
|---|---|---|
| [Szeliski: Computer Vision: Algorithms and Applications, 2nd ed.](https://szeliski.org/Book/) | Reference | The most complete classical + early deep-learning survey, free PDF |
| [Torralba, Isola, Freeman: Foundations of Computer Vision](https://visionbook.mit.edu) | Intermediate | A modern, unified textbook (free online) |
| [Hartley and Zisserman: Multiple View Geometry in Computer Vision](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/hzbook/) | Advanced | The geometry bible: homographies, F/E matrices, triangulation |
| [Prince: Understanding Deep Learning](https://udlbook.github.io/udlbook/) | Intermediate | Clear modern deep learning including transformers and diffusion (free PDF) |
| [Zhang et al.: Dive into Deep Learning](https://d2l.ai) | Beginner to intermediate | Interactive code in PyTorch, with CV chapters |
| [Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville: Deep Learning](https://www.deeplearningbook.org) | Theory | Foundations (older, still useful for maths) |
| [Gao et al.: Introduction to Visual SLAM (Gao Xiang)](https://github.com/gaoxiang12/slambook-en) | Intermediate | From camera model to SLAM, with code (English edition) |
| [Lynch and Park: Modern Robotics](http://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics) | Intermediate | Rotations, rigid-body motion and kinematics for robot vision |
| [Deisenroth et al.: Mathematics for Machine Learning](https://mml-book.github.io) | Beginner | The maths you need, free |
| [Huyen: Designing Machine Learning Systems](https://huyenchip.com/books/) | Practitioner | Production ML thinking |
| [Barfoot: State Estimation for Robotics](http://asrl.utias.utoronto.ca/~tdb/) | Advanced | Probabilistic estimation, Lie groups, SLAM theory |

## Blogs and written explanations

- [Lilian Weng's blog](https://lilianweng.github.io): clear explanations of diffusion, contrastive learning, attention, detection.
- [Distill](https://distill.pub): beautifully visual explanations (archived, still excellent).
- [Jay Alammar: The Illustrated Transformer](https://jalammar.github.io/illustrated-transformer/) and his other illustrated guides.
- [Andrej Karpathy's blog and "Recipe for training neural networks"](https://karpathy.github.io/2019/04/25/recipe/).
- [PyImageSearch](https://pyimagesearch.com) and [LearnOpenCV](https://learnopencv.com): practical OpenCV and deep-learning tutorials with code.
- [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog): model releases explained (SAM, SigLIP, Depth Anything, VLMs).

## Video channels

- [Andrej Karpathy](https://www.youtube.com/@AndrejKarpathy): "Neural Networks: Zero to Hero" builds everything from scratch.
- [Yannic Kilcher](https://www.youtube.com/@YannicKilcher): paper walk-throughs.
- [3Blue1Brown](https://www.3blue1brown.com): visual intuition for linear algebra, calculus and neural networks.
- [Computerphile](https://www.youtube.com/@Computerphile): accessible explanations of computing and AI concepts.
- Conference talk playlists from CVPR, ICCV and ECCV workshops (search the conference channels).

## How to use these resources

1. **Match the lesson**: when this course introduces a topic, read the linked resource for a second explanation in a different style.
2. **Derive and code**: after reading, close the page and re-derive the formula or re-implement the algorithm.
3. **Teach it**: explain the topic to a friend or write a one-page note. If you cannot, you do not yet understand it.
4. **Revisit**: spaced repetition: re-read your notes after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.

:::keypoints
- Choose one main course (CS231n), one reference book (Szeliski), and one geometry resource (Hartley and Zisserman or Stachniss) and actually finish them.
- Use video lectures for intuition, books for depth, and your own code for understanding.
- Re-implement algorithms from scratch to make them stick; teach them to cement them.
:::
