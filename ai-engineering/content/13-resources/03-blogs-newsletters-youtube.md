Quality beats quantity. A handful of sources that **show their work** (experiments, failures, numbers) will teach you more than a firehose of announcements. Below is a starter set; follow a few, drop what is not useful, and keep your list short.

## Practitioner blogs (how to build and operate)

- [Applied LLMs](https://applied-llms.org) — tactical, operational and strategic lessons from a group of experienced builders.
- [Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/) — evals, error analysis, fine-tuning and field guides to improving AI products.
- [Eugene Yan](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/) — patterns for LLM systems, evals, recommender and applied ML.
- [Chip Huyen](https://huyenchip.com/blog/) — AI engineering, platforms, evaluation and ML systems.
- [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net) — near-daily hands-on notes on models, tools and security.
- [Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering) — how Anthropic builds agents, tools, evals and context.
- [Hugging Face blog](https://huggingface.co/blog) — open models, libraries, training and inference how-tos.
- [Thinking Machines: Connectionism](https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/) — rigorous posts on LoRA, determinism and training.

## Research explainers and deep dives

- [Lilian Weng: Lil'Log](https://lilianweng.github.io) — long-form surveys on agents, prompting, hallucination, diffusion and more.
- [Sebastian Raschka: Ahead of AI](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com) — architectures, training methods and paper reviews.
- [Jay Alammar](https://jalammar.github.io) — illustrated explanations of transformers, embeddings and retrieval.
- [Interconnects (Nathan Lambert)](https://www.interconnects.ai) — post-training, RLHF, open models and the industry.
- [Google DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/) — research and model releases with accessible summaries.

## A low-noise routine

1. **Weekly**: skim one newsletter and the changelogs of the tools you use (about 30 minutes).
2. **Weekly**: read one practitioner post fully and try the idea on a small task.
3. **Monthly**: watch one long talk or lecture; read one paper using the three-pass method.
4. **Quarterly**: prune your list; add one new source from a different area.
5. **Always**: prefer sources with **numbers, code and failures** over claims.

## Spotting good sources

Good signs: shows **experiments and negative results**, links to code or data, explains **trade-offs**, cites sources, updates when wrong. Warning signs: breathless "this changes everything" headlines, benchmark screenshots without methodology, product promotion presented as research, no discussion of limitations.

```quiz
? What distinguishes a high-quality practitioner source?
+ It shows experiments, numbers, code and failures, and explains trade-offs
- It posts every announcement within minutes
- It uses the most hype
```

:::keypoints
- Follow a few sources that show their work: practitioner blogs, research explainers, one or two newsletters, a podcast, and selected YouTube channels.
- Match sources to needs: evals, agents, security, inference, post-training, fundamentals.
- Keep a light routine (weekly skim and one deep read) and prune regularly.
- Prefer evidence, code and trade-offs over hype and benchmark screenshots.
:::
